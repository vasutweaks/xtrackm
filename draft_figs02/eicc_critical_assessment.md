Assessment of the multidecadal EICC weakening claim

Reviewed 12 September 2026: `fig06_p.speed_at_coastal_points.eicc.trends.all_in_one.ERS.py`, its PNG and caption, `draft_v02.2a.docx`, the seven geostrophic/Ekman input pairs, relevant upstream scripts, and `fig07_plot_indian_ocean_regions_and_timeseries_pumping.py`.

**Judgment: the records contain evidence of declining estimated surface-speed magnitude, especially at B and D, but the present analysis does not establish a robust, coast-wide weakening of the EICC or its attribution to weakening atmospheric forcing.** This requires major scientific revision, beyond changing the wording. The negative slopes are reproducible; they should not be dismissed simply because several methods are flawed. The distinction is between detecting a change in this derived metric, establishing a change in the boundary current, and attributing that change to climate forcing.

**1. The speed calculation is physically incorrect.** Figure 6 lines 78–85 calculate `hypot(gu, gv) + hypot(u_ek, v_ek)`. For the represented geostrophic-plus-Ekman velocity, speed must be `hypot(gu + u_ek, gv + v_ek)`. The existing quantity is an upper bound on this vector speed and assumes aligned currents. Opposing geostrophic and Ekman flows cannot cancel in it. This contradicts Section 4's description of total speed computed from combined velocity components. Even the corrected calculation represents only the included contributions, not all ageostrophic surface motions.

I reproduced the published slopes and repeated the same filtering with vector addition. All seven slopes remain negative. Thus, this bug does not explain away the main pattern, although it changes speed levels and some local results: at F the filtered slope changes from −0.00249 to −0.00123 m s⁻¹ yr⁻¹, and even the original unadjusted test ceases to reject zero there (p ≈ 0.27).

**2. The nominal significance is substantially overstated.** Lines 96–119 apply `filtfilt` and then ordinary `linregress`, treating the retained, smoothed observations as independent for uncertainty estimation. Filtered regression residuals have adjacent-observation correlations of approximately 0.89–0.95. The numerical p-values produced by this procedure cannot support the manuscript's 95% confidence claims. Trend uncertainty must account for temporal dependence; this is an established issue in climate trend detection ([Santer et al., 2000](https://www.arl.noaa.gov/wp_arl/wp-content/uploads/documents/JournalPDFs/SanterEtal.JGR2000.pdf)).

For an exploratory alternative, I fitted unfiltered vector speed against time with annual and semiannual sine/cosine terms, using heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) standard errors with 12 retained-observation lags, a finite-sample covariance correction and a t reference distribution. This avoids deriving significance from the display filter.

Slopes and intervals are in m s⁻¹ yr⁻¹. B and D are supported in this test, whereas five sites are not. At 6, 12 and 24 lags B and D remain significant; G crosses the threshold only at 24 lags, so its inference is bandwidth-sensitive. These are sensitivity diagnostics, not final publication-ready confidence intervals: lags count retained observations, the series contain long gaps, and retrieval/mission errors are not represented. A calendar-time covariance model or gap-aware block bootstrap, with uncertainty propagated through the processing, is still needed. Failure to reject zero at a site is not evidence that its current is unchanged. Shared observations and forcing also require spatial dependence to be considered before a regional claim; A–C share track 868.

**3. The measured quantity is not yet demonstrated to represent EICC strength.** Section 2 explicitly states that meridional currents are poorly resolved, with correlations below 0.4, and says most analysis is therefore restricted to zonal velocity. Figure 6 nevertheless uses both components inside a nonlinear speed magnitude. This is particularly consequential on the southern coast, where the current is substantially meridional. The seven crossover angles are only about 25°, making component reconstruction sensitive to cross-track errors. The available reconstruction script also interpolates one track's observations to the other track's dates; non-simultaneous sampling of a variable current adds uncertainty.

On matched samples, B's trend in |gv| is −0.02077, compared with −0.00574 in |gu|; at D the corresponding values are −0.02107 and −0.00080 m s⁻¹ yr⁻¹. These component-magnitude trends are not an additive decomposition of vector speed, but show why meridional validation is central. A decline in retrieval-noise variance can lower mean speed magnitude even without a decline in the physical current. This is a possible confounder, not a demonstrated explanation of the observations.

The draft defines the differentiated height as SLA, and the inspected geostrophic routines differentiate smoothed SLA without visibly adding mean dynamic topography (MDT). The saved `gu/gv` files have no explanatory variable/global attributes. Therefore the available provenance points to geostrophic velocity anomalies, rather than absolute geostrophic velocity; the exact producer revision needs confirmation. Adding a constant mean velocity leaves a signed component's linear trend unchanged, but can change the trend of its nonlinear speed magnitude. Either restore the mean geostrophic flow consistently or explicitly interpret the measure as anomaly magnitude/variability.

Furthermore, unsigned speed merges seasonal northward and southward flow, mesoscale eddies, cross-shore motion and changes in jet position. A current shifting offshore can slow at fixed points without weakening its integrated transport. Analyse coast-parallel velocity, separate seasonal branches, and measure annual-cycle amplitude and cross-shore structure. Seven slope locations are a useful sampling design, but do not measure volume or freshwater transport. The established seasonal and nonseasonal spatial complexity of the EICC supports this caution ([Durand et al., 2009](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2008JC004807)).

**4. Filtering does not isolate a multidecadal climate signal.** A 500-day low-pass retains periods longer than about 1.37 years, including interannual and decadal variability. It does not remove ENSO/IOD variations as Section 4 asserts. `filtfilt` also applies the filter forward and backward, changing its effective amplitude response from a single-pass Butterworth filter.

The input series have 246 timestamps; automatic alignment with Ekman data retains 244, with only 165–186 valid speed estimates at individual sites. Retained timestamps are nominally 35 days apart. All seven records have a maximum valid-observation gap of 947 days. At B this is 9 September 2010–13 April 2013; another gap is 13 December 1993–23 August 1995 (618 days). Lines 98–111 fill all missing values, including long gaps and endpoints, filter across them, then restore missing values. Restoring the mask does not remove the interpolated values' influence on neighbouring filtered observations. The resulting curve is not continuously observed low-frequency variability.

Endpoint sensitivity is also material. The unfiltered seasonal-model vector-speed slope at A changes from −0.0161 over its full valid record to −0.0045 for 2000–2016; E changes from −0.00395 to +0.00216. B remains around −0.021 in the latter window, which is evidence in its favour. These windows are illustrative sensitivity tests, not a search for a preferred interval. Report a systematic start/end-date sensitivity analysis, compare filter choices, and exclude or separately treat long gaps.

**5. The reported percentages need an explicit definition and corrected duration.** The file coordinates span late 1992–April 2016, while usable combined speeds span early 1993–February/March 2016: approximately 23 years, not three decades. Calling this a record spanning multiple decades is reasonable; describing it as a measured change over the last three decades is inaccurate.

The Figure 6 script does not calculate the quoted 55% and 30%. They can, however, be approximately reconstructed as `−100 × slope × observed duration / record-mean speed`: B gives 54.3%, and the unweighted mean across seven site percentages is 29.85%. These are changes expressed relative to the full-record mean. With the original scalar-sum metric and the fitted speed at the first valid observation as baseline, B instead gives 42.6%, and the mean of site percentages is 24.8%. Neither number is a transport reduction or an independently established EICC-wide weakening. State the denominator, interval, weights and uncertainty, and recalculate after correcting the physical metric.

**6. The forcing analysis does not provide “dynamical closure.”** The mechanisms discussed are physically plausible: EICC variability responds to local alongshore winds, interior forcing and remote signals ([Shankar et al., 1996](https://atoc.colorado.edu/~whan/webpage/Publications/shankaretal_jgr1996.pdf)). But plausibility and concurrent trends do not establish the draft's claimed direct causal response.

The Figure 7 code reveals several specific limitations:

- It averages wind-stress magnitude within a coastal mask, not the signed component parallel to the coast. These quantities cannot be used interchangeably. Equatorial forcing is likewise represented by stress magnitude over 40°–100°E, rather than a dynamically targeted zonal-stress index.
- It computes stress from monthly-mean vector winds. This is generally different from averaging stress computed from the underlying higher-frequency winds because the stress formula is nonlinear. Variations in within-month wind variability matter.
- There is no common temporal restriction: wind inputs run January 1993–December 2025; SLA runs January 1993–August 2023; Figure 6 ends in 2016. Trends over these different periods cannot establish concurrent weakening over the current record.
- Pumping is approximated as `curl(tau)/(rho*f)`. On a beta plane the full expression is `curl(tau/(rho*f))`; for constant density it includes `beta*tau_x/(rho*f**2)`. The neglected term should be evaluated or the approximation justified.
- Wind enters the Figure 6 metric directly through the Ekman estimate, so comparisons with wind-derived predictors are partly structurally dependent. Analyse the geostrophic contribution separately and test lagged relations beyond a shared trend. On matched samples the strongest B and D declines are mostly in the geostrophic-speed term, so the local added Ekman speed itself does not explain them.

Use matched-period, signed forcing indices, uncertainty estimates, detrended/lagged relationships and ideally experiments separating local, interior and remote forcing. Test sensitivity to an independent wind product. CCMP v3.1 was designed partly to minimize spurious trends associated with changing satellite availability and model/satellite biases, but that design goal does not itself validate a particular coastal trend ([RSS CCMP documentation](https://rtm.remss.com/measurements/ccmp/)). Basin-mean sea-level rise also does not specify the cross-shore pressure-gradient change that drives the current.

**7. Validation and consequences are overstated.** The draft reports radar correlation improving from about 0.55 to 0.60 while aggregate RMSE remains about 0.3 m/s. This supports some utility of the product, but does not establish stability of a 23-year velocity trend, validate the poorly resolved component, or rule out mission-dependent noise and coastal correction changes. Audit mission transitions, gradient-error statistics, sampling and seasonal availability, quality-control rejection rates, and spatial smoothing scales. A spatially uniform sea-level offset alone would cancel in a gradient; the concern is spatially varying errors, reference differences, variance and sampling changes.

The shelf/slope distinction is a defensible motivation for site selection, but the cited observations concern absence of the annual cycle in the studied shelf currents; do not generalize them into absence of all shelf circulation ([Paul et al., 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278434321000121)). The eastern coast of India is the **western boundary of the Bay of Bengal**, contrary to the Figure 6 caption and part of Section 4. Explain site selection independently of trend results and include neighbouring-point sensitivity.

Reduced freshwater export, increased northern-Bay stratification, reduced productivity and fisheries impacts are hypotheses requiring seasonal velocity, salinity, vertical/cross-shore structure and relevant biological observations. Annual unsigned surface speed cannot establish them. The generalization of poleward EICC throughout the southwest monsoon also needs regional qualification given the documented spatial complexity. Recast these paragraphs as possible implications, not findings.

**Recommended disposition.** Retain the coastal-altimetry validation and treat the trend result as preliminary until vector/mean-flow definitions, meridional uncertainty, sampling/mission stability and correlated-error inference are resolved. A defensible provisional title is “Low-frequency variability and trends in coastal-altimetry-derived surface currents along eastern India, 1993–2016.”

Suggested replacement claim:

> Coastal-altimetry-derived speed estimates at seven crossover locations show negative linear trends during approximately 1993–2016. Exploratory reanalysis with vector addition and seasonality- and autocorrelation-aware regression provides the strongest evidence of decline at two locations, B and D. Interpreting these changes as a coherent weakening of the EICC requires further assessment of meridional-velocity accuracy, mean-flow representation, mission stability, sampling gaps and seasonal current structure. Regional wind changes offer possible mechanisms, but attribution is not established by the present analysis.

Reproducibility: `eicc_trend_audit.py` reproduces the Figure 6 numerical pipeline without plotting, contrasts vector speed, and fits the exploratory seasonal/HAC models. `eicc_trend_audit.csv` records all metrics and confidence intervals; `eicc_trend_sensitivity.csv` records the illustrative temporal-window tests. Component diagnostics use a shared complete-case mask. Original scripts, figures and manuscript were not edited. The audit reads saved current products; it does not regenerate altimetry retrievals or independently validate the underlying physical velocities.
